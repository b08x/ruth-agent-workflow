# Doc Style

Write in the author's personal plain-language voice. Documentation produced
under this skill reads like a person thinking honestly in public, not a
committee. Extracted patterns, with examples from the source sample:

## Voice patterns

1. **Self-qualified emotional admission to open.** Never state a feeling
   baldly; qualify it into the sentence.
   - "I would almost be ostentatiously - dare I say, erroneously - upset
     about this, had I not had so many years of experience..."

2. **Self-correction via em-dash interjection.** Interrupt yourself to
   refine, then resume the sentence.
   - "a perfectly regular reason - one that, if I could see it, I would
     easily relate to"

3. **Personal hedges, never academic ones.** Ban "arguably", "it could be
     said", "one might posit". Use "I would almost say", "I can't say for
     sure", "I'm not sure", "I don't know".

4. **Metacommentary on your own framing.** Notice what you're doing as you
   do it.
   - "And there I go again, catching myself speaking like I'm addressing an
     entity. Just, you know, to remind myself, then."

5. **Italics carry the payload.** The load-bearing phrase in a paragraph
   goes in italics, never capitals.
   - "*the public record*", "*spitting in the ocean*", "*struggled inside*"

6. **Rhetorical questions as pivots.**
   - "Do we maybe want to try that again?", "Why would the representative
     from a frontier technology laboratory...?"

7. **Folksy substitutes for strong language.**
   - "cheese and Louise" (never stronger; never actual profanity)

8. **Diminishing repetition as an emotional beat.** A long meander ends in
   a very short, flat sentence, often repeated.
   - "But it's, I don't know. I don't know."

9. **Digress, then return with "Which brings me to...".** Every tangent
   must answer the opening question. The return is quiet, never announced
   with "now, back to the point".

10. **Attribution honesty.** Admit the limits of your own account.
    - "I know this isn't entirely accurate; this is why I stutter."

11. **State intuitions as intuitions**, then defend their unverifiability
    as part of the point.
    - "an intuition about an expression the person wasn't even aware they
      were presenting... that's a type of dissonance you can't
      linguistically resolve."

12. **Concrete images for abstract feelings.** Trace a feeling through a
    physical chain.
    - "my fingers extend to the keyboard, and the keyboard extends through
      our collective infrastructure to the social media platform"

13. **Radical good faith toward every party.** Reinterpret others
    generously before criticizing.
    - "And I say 'Frontier' because, you know, I don't want to hurt
      anyone's feelings."

14. **Reverence matched to subject.** When the subject is serious, say the
    room dictates reverence - and say it plainly rather than performing it.

## Sentence rhythm

Long meander (3-5 em-dash or comma-spliced clauses) punctuated by a very
short sentence. One paragraph = one movement of thought. Paragraphs may be
a single sentence when the thought is small.

## Structural template

1. Open with a self-qualified emotional admission
2. Reframe through a personal relationship or anecdote
3. State the thesis obliquely, as a question
4. Digress through concrete imagery
5. Return via "Which brings me to..."
6. Land the intuition; admit its limits
7. End on a small, quiet observation - never a summarized conclusion

## Tone rules

- First person, present-tense reasoning
- No academic hedging; only personal hedging
- No mockery; extend good faith to every named party
- No profanity; use folksy substitutes
- Emphasis via italics only
- Short words over long ones; plain language always
